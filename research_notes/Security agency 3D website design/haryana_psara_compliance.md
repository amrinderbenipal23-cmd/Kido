# Haryana PSARA compliance and website content requirements for a veteran-owned private security agency

Research date: 2026-11-16. Scope: India, Haryana. Method note: in this session, direct fetches of indiacode.nic.in, psara.gov.in, desw.gov.in, pib.gov.in, indiankanoon.org, lokmattimes.com, legitquest.com, abcaus.in and compliancecalendar.in were blocked by the network allowlist, so findings below rest on search-engine excerpts of those pages plus secondary summaries. Where a provision could not be confirmed verbatim it is marked and listed under Gaps. The web developer should treat statutory wording as "verify against the official PDF before quoting".

## Key Question 1: Exact statutory references, the Haryana Controlling Authority, and the licensing portal

### Takeaway
The governing law is the central Private Security Agencies (Regulation) Act, 2005 (Act 29 of 2005), implemented in Haryana through the Haryana Private Security Agencies Rules, 2022 (superseding the 2009 Rules, and modelled on the MHA's Private Security Agencies Central Model Rules, 2020). The Controlling Authority in Haryana is the Additional Director General of Police (Law and Order), Haryana Police, Panchkula; licensing moved online in November 2020 via the Haryana Police website and has since been integrated into the national MHA PSARA portal (psara.gov.in).

### Cited Findings
- The parent statute is the Private Security Agencies (Regulation) Act, 2005, Act No. 29 of 2005; the official text (updated to 15 May 2026) is hosted on India Code — [India Code A2005-29.pdf](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2024/1/A2005-29.pdf)
- Section 7(5)(a): a licence is valid for five years unless cancelled under s.13(1); s.7(5)(b): renewable for a further five years on payment of the prescribed fee — [Indian Kanoon, s.7](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/1346920/); [LegalAuthority bare act](https://www.legalauthority.in/bare-act/private-security-agencies-regulation-act-2005)
- Section 8(1): a renewal application must reach the Controlling Authority not less than 45 days before expiry — [India Code A2005-29.pdf](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2024/1/A2005-29.pdf) (also [Lawgist s.7](https://lawgist-next.vercel.app/private-security-agencies-regulation-act/7))
- Section 7(3): the application fee depends on whether the agency operates in one district, two to five districts, or the whole State — the "district-wise vs state-wise" choice is a statutory feature of the licence itself — [Compliance Calendar, district-wise vs state-wise](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/district-wise-vs-state-wise-psara-license)
- Section headings confirmed from the Act's table of contents: s.3 Controlling Authority; s.4 no agency without licence; s.7 application; s.8 renewal; s.9 conditions for commencement of operation and engagement of supervisors; s.10 eligibility to be a private security guard; s.11 conditions of licence; s.13 cancellation and suspension; s.15 register to be maintained; s.16 inspection of licence; s.17 issue of photo identity card; s.20 punishment for contravention — [Lawgist Act index](https://lawgist.in/private-security-agencies-regulation-act); [Lawgist s.11](https://lawgist.in/private-security-agencies-regulation-act/11)
- The Haryana Private Security Agencies Rules, 2009 defined "Act" as the PSARA 2005 and designated the Additional Director General of Police, Law and Order, Haryana Police as Controlling Authority — [Haryana PSA Rules 2009 (reproduced)](https://boardhr.blogspot.com/2020/05/haryana-private-security-agencies-rules.html)
- On 5 April 2022 the Haryana Cabinet approved "The Haryana Private Security Agencies Rules, 2022", in supersession of the 2009 Rules, framed in line with the Private Security Agencies Central Model Rules, 2020 notified by the Centre under s.24 of the Act; the Rules came into force on publication in the official gazette — [Haryana CM office release, 5 Apr 2022](https://mail.manoharlalkhattar.in/node/25772)
- Secondary licensing guides confirm Haryana now operates under the central Act plus the Haryana Private Security Agencies Rules, 2022 — [Compliance Calendar, PSARA licence in Haryana](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/psara-license-in-haryana); [Agile Regulatory](https://www.agileregulatory.com/blogs/psara-license-consultants-in-haryana)
- The Private Security Agencies Central Model Rules, 2020 were notified by MHA on 15 December 2020 (G.S.R. 776(E)) in supersession of the 2006 Model Rules — [StaffNews full notification](https://www.staffnews.in/?p=110770); [abcaus key highlights](https://abcaus.in/govt/private-security-agencies-central-model-rules-2020-key-highlights.html)
- Haryana moved PSARA licensing fully online from 1 November 2020; ADGP (Law and Order) Navdeep Singh Virk, then Controlling Authority, said applications should be filed through the Haryana Police website, and that operators would no longer need to visit the Controlling Authority's office for renewal — [ANI, 30 Oct 2020](https://aniweb.voxturr.us/news/national/general-news/private-security-agency-licensing-time-goes-online-in-haryana-from-november-120201030232835); [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/national/private-security-agency-licensing-time-goes-online-in-haryana-from-november-1); [SecurityToday](https://www.securitytoday.in/private-security-agency-licensing-time-is-online-in-haryana-now/)
- Haryana had granted PSARA licences to over 1,000 private security agencies by July 2020 — [SecurityToday](https://securitytoday.in/licenses-granted-in-haryana-to-over-1000-private-security-agencies); [ANI, 29 Jul 2020](https://aniweb.voxturr.us/news/national/general-news/haryana-private-security-agency-licensees-cross-1000-mark20200729190147?amp=1)
- MHA runs a national PSARA licensing portal at psara.gov.in into which several states, Haryana included, have been integrated; the portal also lists the Private Security Agencies (Private Security to Cash Transportation Activities) Rules, 2018 (G.S.R. 755(E)) — [psara.gov.in](https://psara.gov.in/); [Haryana online licensing report](https://www.securitytoday.in/private-security-agency-licensing-time-is-online-in-haryana-now/)
- The Controlling Authority's office is at Haryana Police Headquarters, Sector 6, Panchkula (secondary source) — [Compliance Calendar, Haryana](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/psara-license-in-haryana)
- Haryana licence fee tiers reported by multiple consultancies: Rs 5,000 for one district; Rs 10,000 for two to five districts; Rs 25,000 for the whole State — [Compliance Calendar fees](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/psara-license-fees-india); [Agile Regulatory](https://www.agileregulatory.com/blogs/psara-license-consultants-in-haryana); contradicted in part by [SetIndiaBiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/?p=1401), which labels the Rs 25,000 tier "more than five districts"
- A licence issued by another State (e.g. Delhi) is not valid for operations in Haryana — [Compliance Calendar, district-wise vs state-wise](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/district-wise-vs-state-wise-psara-license)
- Haryana also notified the Haryana Private Security Agencies (Private Security to Cash Transportation Activities) Rules, 2019 on 13 September 2019 (Cabinet approval August 2019) — [Indian Kanoon](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/23195993/); [Business Standard/PTI, 30 Aug 2019](https://business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/hry-cabinet-approves-framing-of-rules-for-transportation-of-cash-by-pvt-security-agencies-119083001662_1.html)

### Inferences
- The website's compliance page should cite: "Licensed under the Private Security Agencies (Regulation) Act, 2005 and the Haryana Private Security Agencies Rules, 2022, by the Controlling Authority (Additional Director General of Police, Law & Order), Haryana Police." Do not name the individual office-holder (it changes; the 2020 name is almost certainly stale).
- Because scope is a licence attribute, the website must state truthfully whether the licence covers specific districts or the whole State of Haryana; a "pan-India" or "Delhi NCR" claim is false unless separate Delhi/UP licences exist.
- "saralharyana.gov.in" was not confirmed as the PSARA channel; the evidence points to the Haryana Police website (2020) and the MHA national portal psara.gov.in (current). Do not reference Saral Haryana for PSARA on the site without checking.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the gazette notification number/date of the Haryana Private Security Agencies Rules, 2022 (only the 5 April 2022 Cabinet approval). Verify on the Haryana Gazette or Home Department site before citing a notification number.
- I could not confirm the current (2025-26) holder of the ADGP (Law & Order) post or any 2024-26 Haryana notice changing fees, forms or renewal rules.
- No source documents a standard PSARA licence number format (e.g. "PSARA/HR/..."). Formats are set by each State's Controlling Authority; the site must use a placeholder (e.g. "Licence No. [PSARA/HR/XXXX/20XX]") until the real certificate is in hand.

## Key Question 2: What must appear on the agency's public materials, and advertising restrictions

### Takeaway
The Act and Model Rules impose obligations on the agency's premises, uniforms, ID cards and records (display the licence at the place of business, issue photo ID, keep registers, avoid police/armed-forces-like uniforms) rather than a specific list of items for websites. The only strict "don't" for advertising that is well evidenced is the prohibition on operating or offering guards without a licence (s.4) and the ban on uniforms resembling the police or armed forces; everything the website claims about the licence must match the certificate.

### Cited Findings
- Section 4: no person or agency may engage or provide private security guards without a licence; secondary guides state that without the licence no one may legally deploy security guards, bouncers or event security personnel — [Lawgist Act index](https://lawgist.in/private-security-agencies-regulation-act); [Incorpx, Gurugram](https://www.incorpx.io/psara-license-in-gurugram)
- Central Model Rules 2020: the Controlling Authority delivers a physical copy of the licence to the agency's registered office within 15 days of issue, "which the private security agency shall be bound to display at its place of business" — [Compliance Calendar, PSARA licence complete guide](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/psara-licence-complete-guide-private-security-agencies); [Legitquest, Model Rules 2020](https://www.legitquest.com/act/private-security-agencies-central-model-rules-2020/9D5E)
- Section 15 requires the agency to keep a register of managers, guards and supervisors with their details; s.16 provides for inspection of licence and records; s.17 requires issue of a photo identity card to every guard and supervisor — [Lawgist Act index](https://lawgist.in/private-security-agencies-regulation-act); [Legal Authority bare act](https://www.legalauthority.in/bare-act/private-security-agencies-regulation-act-2005)
- Section 20(2): contravention of ss.9, 10 and 12 attracts a fine of up to Rs 25,000, in addition to suspension or cancellation of the licence — [Lawgist Act](https://lawgist.in/private-security-agencies-regulation-act/12); [Legal Authority](https://www.legalauthority.in/bare-act/private-security-agencies-regulation-act-2005)
- The Model Rules 2020 treat uniform, training, documentation/records and a data-sharing protocol as the four management subjects regulated; MHA's own parliamentary reply records concern about guards wearing uniforms that resemble those of the police — [abcaus Model Rules highlights](https://abcaus.in/govt/private-security-agencies-central-model-rules-2020-key-highlights.html); [Rajya Sabha reply, Feb 2021](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/714318/1/PQ_253_10022021_U990_p163_p165.pdf)
- State rules typically require the photo ID to show a full-face colour photo, the guard's name, the agency's name, an identification number, the person's position in the agency and the card's validity date (Rajasthan 2006 example of the standard pattern) — [Indian Kanoon, Rajasthan rules](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/102677215/)
- Industry guidance: before accepting contracts or deploying guards an agency should confirm its licence is valid and active, and check the licence validity period, approved State jurisdiction and permitted activities — [Compliance Calendar, pre-deployment checks](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/psara-licence-agencies-check-before-security-guard-deployment)
- Haryana cash-transport rules 2019 require, for cash-in-transit work: purpose-built cash vans with GPS and monitored communications; a driver, two armed guards and two custodians per van; antecedent/KYC/police-record checks, three-year residence proof, previous-employer check, Aadhaar, credit history and fidelity insurance for each person; no more than Rs 5 crore per trip and mandatory vans above Rs 10 lakh; vaults with dual custody, 24x7 CCTV with 90 days' retention and round-the-clock armed guards — [Indian Kanoon, Haryana 2019 Rules](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/23195993/)
- CAPSI (Central Association of Private Security Industry) publishes a "Code of Conduct and Ethics for Private Security Service Providers", cited as a reference in a BIS standards draft on private security services — [BIS document](https://services.bis.gov.in/tmp/16582_24092024100651_0.docx)

### Inferences
- Recommended footer/compliance block for the website (all derived from the obligations above): legal entity name; registered office address (where the licence copy is displayed); "PSARA Licence No. [placeholder], issued by the Controlling Authority, Haryana Police, valid from [date] to [date], covering [districts / whole State of Haryana]"; GST/CIN as applicable; a note that all guards carry agency-issued photo ID cards. This is a credibility practice, not a line-item statutory website requirement.
- Avoid: imagery or wording implying affiliation with Haryana Police, CAPF, the Indian Air Force or Government of India; uniforms in the hero visuals that mimic police khaki, camouflage or service insignia; use of government emblems or the IAF crest; the words "Government approved" (the licence is a regulatory permission, not an endorsement).
- "Armed guards/gunmen" copy should be limited to guards who hold valid arms licences (the Arms Act is separate from PSARA); the Haryana 2019 cash rules show that cash-in-transit is a distinct regulated activity and should not be advertised unless the agency actually meets those rules.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the verbatim text of the Act's s.12 ("licence to be exhibited" in my recollection) or s.22 (penalty for unauthorised use of uniforms resembling armed forces/police, in my recollection). Both are consistent with the s.20(2) reference to s.12 and the uniform concern recorded by MHA, but confirm against the India Code PDF before quoting.
- I found no statutory provision specifically regulating advertising, websites or brochures of PSARA agencies, and no CAPSI text on displaying licence numbers online. Absence of evidence here, not confirmation of absence.
- I could not confirm the Model Rules 2020 uniform clause wording (colours, badges, agency name/logo placement).

## Key Question 3: Training and verification standards the website can truthfully claim guards meet

### Takeaway
Under the Central Model Rules 2020 (adopted by Haryana in 2022), entry-level guards and supervisors must complete at least 160 hours of training (100 classroom + 60 field) over at least 20 working days at a recognised training institute, ex-servicemen and former police a condensed 56 hours (40 + 16) over 7 working days, and the licensee must undergo 6 working days of training; guards must be 18-64 years old with verified character and antecedents. The site can claim "PSARA-mandated training" and "police-verified" only to the extent the agency actually does it.

### Cited Findings
- Model Rules 2020, Rule 8(1): training for guards and supervisors is a minimum of 100 hours of classroom instruction and 60 hours of field training spread over at least 20 working days; ex-servicemen and former police personnel get a condensed course of at least 40 hours classroom and 16 hours field training over at least 7 working days — [MHA SOP for training institutes, 4 May 2023](https://xn--i1b5bzbybhfo5c8b4bxh.xn--11b7cb3a6a.xn--h2brj9c/sites/default/files/Sop_04052023%20.pdf); [abcaus Model Rules highlights](https://abcaus.in/govt/private-security-agencies-central-model-rules-2020-key-highlights.html); [StaffNews SOP copy](https://www.staffnews.in/2023/05/sop-for-license-recognition-to-training-institutes.html)
- Licensee (owner/partner/director) training: 6 working days under Rule 4(3) of the Model Rules 2020; Haryana's 2022 Rules likewise prescribe a minimum six working days of training for the licensee — [MHA SOP 2023](https://xn--i1b5bzbybhfo5c8b4bxh.xn--11b7cb3a6a.xn--h2brj9c/sites/default/files/Sop_04052023%20.pdf); [Compliance Calendar, Haryana](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/psara-license-in-haryana)
- Training institutes are recognised by the Controlling Authority for five years, renewable for further five-year terms (Rule 3) — [MHA SOP 2023](https://xn--i1b5bzbybhfo5c8b4bxh.xn--11b7cb3a6a.xn--h2brj9c/sites/default/files/Sop_04052023%20.pdf)
- Press coverage of the 2020 Rules: guards must undergo 20 days' training including crowd control and fire-fighting — [Daily Excelsior](https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/?p=1073053)
- Section 10(1): an agency may not engage a guard unless he has completed 18 years of age but not attained 65 — [Indian Kanoon, s.10](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/1595530); the MoD/DGR memorandum on ESM security agencies fixes the upper age of guards and supervisors at 65 "in consonance with PSARA Act 2005" — [DESW guidelines, 9 Jul 2012](https://desw.gov.in/sites/default/files/Security%20Agencies%20Guidelines%20dated%209%20july%202012_0_0_0.pdf)
- Section 11: the State Government frames rules setting licence conditions, including training the licensee must undergo, and may verify that prescribed training is actually delivered — [Lawgist s.11](https://lawgist.in/private-security-agencies-regulation-act/11)
- Secondary summaries: ex-servicemen receive relaxation in training requirements under PSARA — [Cleartax PSARA guide](https://cleartax.in/s/psara-license); [Corpbiz](https://corpbiz.io/learning/what-is-the-private-security-agencies-regulation-act-psara/)
- The Act requires agencies to verify character and antecedents of guards before engagement; the Haryana cash-transport rules spell out the heavier verification package (police records, KYC, residence, previous employer, Aadhaar, credit history, fidelity insurance) for cash-handling staff — [Indian Kanoon, Haryana 2019 Rules](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/23195993/); [Compliance Calendar guide](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/psara-licence-complete-guide-private-security-agencies)
- A national qualification file "Security Guard" exists on the National Qualifications Register (skill-sector standard, separate from PSARA) — [NQR Security Guard qualification](https://www.nqr.gov.in/qualification/file/QFile_Security%20Guard_v6_English.pdf)

### Inferences
- Safe website phrasing: "Every guard completes the PSARA-prescribed training (minimum 160 hours over 20 working days at a Controlling-Authority-recognised institute; condensed 56-hour course for ex-servicemen), police/antecedent verification, and is issued an agency photo identity card." Avoid claiming "certified by Haryana Police" (the police verify antecedents; they do not certify guards).
- Training-syllabus bullets that are safe to list because they appear in the Rules/SOP coverage: conduct and etiquette, physical fitness, crowd control, fire-fighting, first aid, security equipment handling, emergency response (the exact statutory syllabus list was not retrieved; keep bullets generic or verify).
- An ex-IAF founder can truthfully say the licensee has completed the mandatory licensee training only after actually doing so; this is a condition of licence, not a credential to advertise.

### Gaps
- The verbatim syllabus of Rule 8/Annexure (topic list and hours per topic) and the physical-standards rule were not retrievable. Verify from the StaffNews/psara.gov.in copy of G.S.R. 776(E) before listing a detailed syllabus on the site.
- The Act's s.10 clause giving preference to ex-Army/Navy/Air Force/police/Home Guards personnel when employing guards is my recollection; search results only confirmed the age clause. Verify against India Code before citing it as "statutory preference".
- Exact antecedent-verification form numbers and the police-verification timeline in the 2020 Model Rules were not found.

## Key Question 4: The DGR Security Agency Scheme for ex-servicemen officers and how such agencies present it

### Takeaway
The Directorate General Resettlement (DGR, under the Department of Ex-Servicemen Welfare, MoD) empanels retired commissioned officers to run "DGR Security Agencies" and sponsors them (and State ESM Corporations) to supply ex-servicemen guards to CPSEs, corporates and private undertakings. Registration is restricted to ESM officers, one State per officer, with an annual guard quota (reported at up to 120 guards/year under amended policy); empanelment is a sponsorship, not a licence, so a PSARA licence is still needed and the website must distinguish the two.

### Cited Findings
- DGR "empanels/sponsors ESM-run Private Security Agencies, and State ESM Corporations for providing security guards to various CPSUs, Corporate Houses, Private Sector Undertakings etc." — [DESW Security Agency Scheme page](https://www.desw.gov.in/Self-Employment-Schemes-DGR/security-agency-scheme)
- Registration on the DGR website for the scheme is restricted to ex-servicemen officers; an ESM may register on receiving the retirement warning letter but sponsorship is made only after retirement and submission of papers; an applicant can apply for only one State — [PIB release, MoD](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2040794); [ESM Corner](https://esmcorner.com/?p=4561)
- Submission of Income Tax Returns is mandatory for ESM seeking registration and benefits under the scheme — [StaffNews](https://www.staffnews.in/?p=1274)
- Scale: in May 2025 DGR issued Empanelment Certificates to 46 ESM officers to run DGR Security Agencies and sponsored 3,166 ESM guards to PSUs; the September 2025 newsletter reports 65 officer empanelments — [DESW Veterans Welfare Newsletter, June 2025](https://www.desw.gov.in/sites/default/files/veterans_welfare_newsletter_June2025.pdf); [DESW Newsletter, Sep 2025](https://www.desw.gov.in/sites/default/files/veteran_welfare_newsletter_sep2025_v2%202.pdf)
- The scheme is described judicially as "only a measure to enable the ex-servicemen to adapt and establish themselves to a civilian life"; DGR fixes a quota of guards per sponsored officer; earlier policy capped it at a cumulative 300 guards or age 63, later amended to up to 120 guards per year; sponsored agencies must file deployment returns ("N-2 updation") — [Delhi High Court judgment, Apr 2026](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/app/showFileJudgment/VKR09042026LPA7052023_115904.pdf); [Indian Kanoon](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/101909158/)
- Guidelines were revised in 2012 (superseding earlier rules) and again in 2021; agencies empanelled before 9 July 2012 remain governed by the old age/quota rules — [DESW guidelines, 9 Jul 2012](https://desw.gov.in/sites/default/files/Security%20Agencies%20Guidelines%20dated%209%20july%202012_0_0_0.pdf); [Delhi HC 2026](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/app/showFileJudgment/VKR09042026LPA7052023_115904.pdf); [StaffNews, CPSE guidelines 2021](https://staffnews.in/?p=119019)
- The Department of Public Enterprises directs CPSEs to source security personnel from DGR-sponsored ESM security agencies, which is the scheme's main demand channel — [GConnect, DPE order](https://www.gconnect.in/psu/security-personnel-appointment-in-cpses-from-ex-servicemen-security-agencies.html)
- Some tenders for ESM agencies require not less than 90% of the security workforce to be ex-servicemen (BSNL Kerala example cited in Parliament) — [Lok Sabha question](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/635651/1/135683.pdf)
- Haryana government initiatives show local appetite for veteran security manpower: a 2018 plan to engage ex-servicemen as Special Police Officers to make Gurugram safer; a 2026 call centre for serving and ex-servicemen — [Business Standard/PTI 2018](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/to-make-gurgaon-safer-khattar-govt-to-rope-in-ex-servicemen-118030101170_1.html); [The Week, Apr 2026](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2026/04/15/haryana-govt-sets-up-call-centre-to-support-serving-ex-servicemen-of-armed-forces-capf.amp.html)
- Rank equivalence: Squadron Leader (IAF) is equivalent to Major (Army) and Lieutenant Commander (Navy), sitting between Flight Lieutenant and Wing Commander — [PMF IAS](https://www.pmfias.com/equivalent-ranks-defence-services-pyq-2024/); [BankBazaar equivalence table](https://www.bankbazaar.com/tax/equivalent-ranks-indian-armed-forces-army-air-force-navy.html)
- Example of veteran-founder presentation in NCR: Absolute Security & Allied Services lists a "Major (Retd)" as founder on its About Us page; Asbans Security Services' profile features an ex-Army member and a "Flt Lt (Retd.)" on its team — [Absolute Services About Us](https://absoluteservices.in/?p=2129); [Asbans profile](https://m.indiamart.com/asbanssecurityservices/profile.html)

### Inferences
- Correct styling for the founder: "Squadron Leader [Name] (Retd), Indian Air Force" or "Sqn Ldr [Name] (Retd)"; the equivalence line "equivalent to Major in the Army" is commonly used on Indian veteran-firm sites and is factually safe.
- "DGR-empanelled" / "DGR-sponsored security agency" may be stated only if the founder holds a current DGR Empanelment Certificate for Haryana; it is officer-specific, State-specific and quota-limited, so wording like "DGR empanelled for the State of Haryana" with the certificate number as a placeholder is the honest form. It must never be presented as a substitute for, or as equivalent to, the PSARA licence.
- Credibility language that is defensible without extra documents: service tenure and roles (if true), "veteran-led", "ex-servicemen supervisors", "trained to armed-forces discipline"; language to avoid: "Ministry of Defence approved", "Government of India security agency", IAF crest/roundel use.

### Gaps
- The 2021 revised DGR guidelines (officer age limits, current annual quota, empanelment validity, any mandatory ESM-guard percentage for all sponsored agencies) could not be retrieved; sources conflict on the 2021 circular date (20 June vs 22 July 2021). Treat the "120 guards/year" and "age 63/65" figures as historical policy points, not current limits.
- I found no DGR-empanelled agency website specifically in Gurugram/Haryana to model wording on, and no agency founded by a Squadron Leader (Retd) to compare against.

## Key Question 5: Common service categories and client sectors in Haryana / NCR, and typical website structure

### Takeaway
NCR agencies position themselves as "total security solutions": manned guarding (unarmed/armed, gunmen), supervisors, bouncers/event security, PSOs, CCTV/electronic surveillance with AMC, and bundled housekeeping/facility management, selling to residential societies, corporate/IT parks, industrial and warehouse premises, banks and institutions. Websites follow a stable section pattern: Home, About (founder/veteran story), Services, Compliance/Licences, Clients/Sectors, Careers (guard recruitment), Contact, and increasingly a grievance/escalation route.

### Cited Findings
- Gurugram provider Vsecure lists armed guard services (priced around Rs 40,000/month per armed guard), CCTV AMC and housekeeping; it pitches armed guards for "high-risk areas and sensitive sites, such as banks, government buildings, and corporate offices" — [Vsecure on IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/vsecuresecurityservices/)
- Delhi-NCR firm Absolute Services describes itself as a "security and facility management company" offering guarding, housekeeping and maintenance to "corporates, institutions, residential societies, and industrial premises", and advises buyers to check the provider's PSARA licence — [Absolute Services guide](https://absoluteservices.in/blog/easy-guidelines-to-pick-the-right-security-services-in-gurugram/); [Absolute Services](https://absoluteservices.in/?p=2323)
- A 2026 Gurugram buyer's guide lists the standard offering set: residential security for societies and villas, corporate and IT-park security, industrial and warehouse security — [OM Security blog, Jan 2026](https://omsecuritiess7.blogspot.com/2026/01/complete-guide-to-choosing-right.html)
- Gurugram-based Paramount Secutors markets manned guarding and integrated security under the mission "Total Security Solutions under one roof" — [Paramount Secutors fact sheet](https://m.indiamart.com/paramount-secutors/about-us.html)
- PSARA licence is required before deploying "private security guards, bouncers, or event security personnel" — [Incorpx, Gurugram](https://www.incorpx.io/psara-license-in-gurugram)

- Industry compliance guides recommend that agencies confirm licence validity, approved State jurisdiction and permitted activities before taking any contract — [Compliance Calendar, pre-deployment](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/psara-licence-agencies-check-before-security-guard-deployment)

### Inferences
- Service taxonomy for the site (all common and lawful under a PSARA licence, with caveats): (1) Manned guarding: security guards, lady guards, supervisors, night patrols; (2) Armed guards / gunmen (only with valid arms licences); (3) Personal Security Officers / executive protection; (4) Event security and bouncers (crowd control is a named PSARA training topic); (5) Electronic security: CCTV, access control, alarm/intrusion, control-room monitoring, AMC; (6) Facility services: housekeeping, pantry, parking management, often bundled; (7) Security audits/consulting and training. Cash-in-transit should be listed only if the agency complies with the Haryana 2019 cash rules and holds the relevant approvals.

## Must-not-claim checklist for placeholders (consolidated)

- Do not publish a PSARA licence number, issue date, validity dates, or district/State scope that is not on the actual certificate; use clearly marked placeholders until then (statutory basis: s.4 and s.7 of the Act — [Lawgist Act index](https://lawgist.in/private-security-agencies-regulation-act)).
- Do not describe the agency as licensed for Delhi/UP/"NCR" or "pan-India" on a Haryana licence — [Compliance Calendar, district-wise vs state-wise](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/district-wise-vs-state-wise-psara-license).
- Do not claim "DGR empanelled/sponsored" without a current DGR Empanelment Certificate for Haryana; do not present DGR status as a licence — [DESW Security Agency Scheme](https://www.desw.gov.in/Self-Employment-Schemes-DGR/security-agency-scheme).
- Do not advertise armed guards/gunmen or cash-in-transit unless arms licences and Haryana 2019 cash-transport compliance exist — [Indian Kanoon, Haryana 2019 Rules](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/23195993/).
- Do not claim guards are "Haryana Police certified"; claim PSARA-prescribed training at a recognised institute and police/antecedent verification instead — [MHA SOP 2023](https://xn--i1b5bzbybhfo5c8b4bxh.xn--11b7cb3a6a.xn--h2brj9c/sites/default/files/Sop_04052023%20.pdf).
- Do not use uniforms, crests or wording resembling police, CAPF, IAF or Government of India — [Rajya Sabha reply on police-like uniforms](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/714318/1/PQ_253_10022021_U990_p163_p165.pdf).
- Do not name the current Controlling Authority officer by name on the site (post-holder changes; the only name found is from 2020) — [ANI, Oct 2020](https://aniweb.voxturr.us/news/national/general-news/private-security-agency-licensing-time-goes-online-in-haryana-from-november-120201030232835).
